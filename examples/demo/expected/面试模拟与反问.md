## Role-specific simulated questions

### Direct product leader

#### 1. What would your first 60 days of billing discovery look like?

- **Assessment:** ability to frame discovery without inventing company-specific facts.
- **Strategy:** separate a proposed future plan from past evidence; begin with workflows, stakeholders, and baseline definitions.
- **Example answer:** “I would first map the invoice and renewal workflows with finance operations and support, then interview customers representing different billing patterns. At ExamplePay, 12 interviews helped me distinguish three correction workflows before I prioritized one shared flow. I would define baseline metrics with data partners before proposing a roadmap, because the right baseline at CloudExample is not yet known.”
- **Basis:** PRJ-001; future actions are explicitly framed as a plan.
- **Possible follow-up:** “What would you defer until after the first 60 days?” — Avoid committing to a roadmap before workflow and baseline discovery; explain that sequencing depends on evidence [PRJ-001].

#### 2. What did you personally own in the invoice redesign?

- **Assessment:** role boundaries and individual contribution.
- **Strategy:** distinguish product discovery and roadmap decisions from engineering and metric validation.
- **Example answer:** “I led the 12 customer interviews, workflow mapping, prioritization, and roadmap decisions. I also coordinated the 40-account pilot and used feedback to adjust permissions and confirmation steps. Engineers owned implementation, and a data analyst owned final metric validation.”
- **Basis:** PRJ-001, CAP-002.
- **Possible follow-ups:** “Which decision did you make alone?” — Limit the answer to product prioritization and roadmap decisions [PRJ-001]. “What did engineering own?” — State clearly that engineers owned implementation [CAP-002].

#### 3. Why did you consolidate three correction workflows?

- **Assessment:** problem framing and trade-offs.
- **Strategy:** connect repeated manual work to the shared-flow decision and mention migration constraints.
- **Example answer:** “The interviews and workflow mapping showed that finance administrators repeated correction work across three separate flows. A shared flow addressed that repetition, but existing invoice records had to remain readable, so we used a phased launch rather than replacing everything at once.”
- **Basis:** PRJ-001.
- **Possible follow-ups:** “What alternative did you reject?” — The evidence supports rejecting a single immediate replacement, not other unrecorded options [PRJ-001]. “What would make you change that decision?” — Discuss evidence about record readability or pilot risk without claiming an unrecorded event [PRJ-001].

#### 4. What is your main gap for this role?

- **Assessment:** self-awareness and evidence boundaries.
- **Strategy:** name the real gap and avoid claiming a hidden strength.
- **Example answer:** “My clearest gap is advanced SQL. I can explore product questions with basic queries, but I rely on analysts for validation and do not claim production data-engineering expertise. My stronger evidence is in billing discovery and cross-functional delivery.”
- **Basis:** CAP-003, PRJ-002.
- **Possible follow-up:** “How would you handle a data question without an analyst?” — Limit the answer to basic exploration and state that final validation would need an appropriate review mechanism [CAP-003].

### Key partner: finance operations or data

#### 5. What does the 18% result actually mean?

- **Assessment:** metric literacy and resistance to exaggeration.
- **Strategy:** state object, direction, comparison period, source, and what the metric does not prove.
- **Example answer:** “Manual invoice corrections were 18% lower during the first three months after general availability than during the three months before launch, based on the fictional ExamplePay operations dashboard. It measures correction volume, not revenue or customer satisfaction, and I would avoid claiming either without separate evidence.”
- **Basis:** PRJ-001, EVD-001.
- **Possible follow-ups:** “Who validated the metric?” — A data analyst owned final metric validation [PRJ-001]. “Can you attribute the full change to the redesign?” — No; retain the measured comparison without claiming sole causality [EVD-001].

#### 6. How did you use SQL in the subscription retry project?

- **Assessment:** technical depth and honesty about the SQL requirement.
- **Strategy:** demonstrate working use while preserving the analyst-review boundary.
- **Example answer:** “I wrote basic exploratory queries to examine failed-renewal reasons by segment. A data analyst checked the queries and validated the results before we prioritized two retry-rule changes. I would describe my SQL as working-level exploration, not advanced production analytics.”
- **Basis:** PRJ-002, CAP-003.
- **Possible follow-up:** “What did the analyst change?” — The master records review and validation but not specific edits; mark that detail as pending rather than inventing it [PRJ-002].

#### 7. How did the phased launch change the product?

- **Assessment:** learning and adaptation.
- **Strategy:** identify concrete changes made from pilot feedback.
- **Example answer:** “The 40-account pilot exposed the need to adjust permissions and confirmation steps before general availability. Those changes were product refinements based on observed feedback; the phased approach also allowed finance operations to preserve access to existing invoice records.”
- **Basis:** PRJ-001.
- **Possible follow-up:** “What did not change?” — The shared correction-flow direction remained; recorded changes concern permissions and confirmation steps [PRJ-001].

### HR

#### 8. Why move from internal support workflows to billing products?

- **Assessment:** career logic and motivation.
- **Strategy:** connect the common workflow-discovery thread without inventing emotional or company-specific claims.
- **Example answer:** “At ExampleRetail, I learned to map support workflows and evaluate operational changes through pilots. Since joining ExamplePay, I have applied that approach to invoicing and subscription renewals. This role brings together the workflow-discovery experience and the B2B billing work reflected in my recent projects.”
- **Basis:** EXP-001, EXP-002, PRJ-001, PRJ-003.
- **Possible follow-up:** “Why CloudExample specifically?” — Connect the stated billing and subscription scope to real invoice and renewal work; do not invent knowledge of internal culture or strategy [PRJ-001, PRJ-002].

#### 9. Do the location and availability requirements work for you?

- **Assessment:** basic condition alignment and consistency.
- **Strategy:** answer only from confirmed preferences and availability.
- **Example answer:** “Berlin or remote work in Europe fits my stated preference. My recorded availability is eight weeks after accepting an offer. I would confirm the exact arrangement before proceeding rather than assume a different start date.”
- **Basis:** EXP profile and Preferences sections in career-master.md.
- **Possible follow-up:** “Is the eight-week period flexible?” — The master does not establish flexibility; mark it for personal confirmation.

## Opening preparation

### Approximately 60-second self-introduction

“I am a product manager with experience since July 2019, focused recently on B2B SaaS billing and subscription workflows. At ExamplePay, I led discovery for an invoice correction redesign, including 12 customer interviews and workflow mapping with finance operations. We prioritized a shared correction flow and used a 40-account pilot to refine permissions and confirmation steps before launch. Manual corrections were 18% lower in the first three months after general availability than in the preceding three months, with final metric validation owned by a data analyst. I also worked on subscription retry rules, where I used basic exploratory SQL with analyst review. My strongest fit for this role is the combination of billing discovery, cross-functional delivery, and careful use of evidence.” [EXP-001, PRJ-001, PRJ-002]

### Why this role

“This role combines the billing and subscription problems I have already worked on with the discovery and cross-functional delivery approach reflected in my recent projects. I see a direct connection to my invoice and renewal work, while the JD's SQL preference is an area where I can contribute basic exploration but should remain clear about my current boundary.” [PRJ-001, PRJ-002, CAP-003]
